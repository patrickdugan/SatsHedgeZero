## Tariff and scheduler

SHZ candidate tariff (not a claim about Lighter's current schedule): 0.2 bps maker / 2 bps taker = 20 / 200 ppm. Free maker/cancel eligibility 200 ms, free taker 300 ms; priority maker/cancel 0 ms, priority taker 100 ms. The 200 ms advantage is an explicit local scheduling rule. Use router-local trusted monotonic arrival time and FIFO for equal eligibility; price-time priority among resting quotes. No customer-order preview, no hidden privileged order flow, no cross-router global sequencing claim. Private quotes remain local to a chosen router. Initial FOK taker fills one maker quote; partial maker fills supported. Multi-maker sweep, mass quote and live transport remain future gates.

For integer USD quantity Q and execution price p, fee F(Q,p,r)=ceil(Q*1e8*r/(p*1e6)) satoshis. Charge partial makers F(cumulative_after)-F(cumulative_before) at their fixed quoted price, preventing fee inflation through splitting. Signed ppm and satoshi ceilings bind trader intent; no fee on rejection/cancel. Prototype allocation: 70% of fee due to oracle pool (floor in sats), remainder to router, conserving every satoshi. At $100m matched daily notional and 20% priority makers / 20% priority takers, gross theoretical priority revenue is $4400/day before rounding, rebates, subscriptions and costs; actual adoption is unknown. Both rates and split are experimental.

Existing inverse DLC spec remains ZeroFeePolicy::ZERO. Fees are distinct provisional channel obligations, not deducted from DLC payouts and not recognized as realized revenue until authenticated channel updates settle them.

## Trust and reservation

Canonical channel allocation plan is signed by trader and one pinned channel-state authority. It commits network, epoch, policy, funding outpoint, owner, cap, strictly ordered distinct router IDs and exact per-router budgets whose sum is at most cap. Registry rejects duplicate outpoints. Each local router uses only its share; account owner and monotone order sequence checked. This is an immutable single-generation prototype. A malicious authority signing conflicting snapshots, duplicate active processes for one router, real Lightning funding proof, allocation rotation, persistence and recovery need independent channel-state consensus and fencing before deployment. Never call an unverified allocation a live LN deposit.

Balance invariant per allocation: free + resting + pending = cap. Maker reserve is quantity times ceiling of per-unit margin and fee; conservative for arbitrary partial fills. Fill consumes exact per-fill inverse margin and incremental maker fee, releases rounding excess, and holds exact taker margin + fee. All checks precede the single local mutation; no credit creation on reject. Cancellation releases only unfilled quote reserve, cannot release pending fills. This version leaves provisional obligations locked until a future authenticated LN coalescer implements commit/abort/recovery; it cannot withdraw or settle funds. Global sum of distinct router budgets is bounded under the canonical-registry and single-writer assumptions.

Two relay-signed normalized price ticks are authenticated under receiver-pinned research keys. Tick scopes include network, epoch, policy, source, sequence, publication timestamp, price and report digest. Both are fresh within one second at actual execution, skew <=500 ms, relative spread <=50 bps, midpoint floor e8. Match price collar +/-200 bps. Reject rollback, equivocation and duplicate pairs; allow same unchanged tick from one source when the other advances. This demonstrates hot-path relay authentication, not live Pyth/Chainlink source verification, SLA, completeness or settlement authorization. Signed source adapters and separate historical proof pipeline will follow.

## Registered benchmark implementation

Use the exact upstream generator at the pinned commit and run its own Rust throughput example on the same runner. The SHZ adapter maps TL decimal cents c to integer BTC/USD price 100000+5(c-250), and TL thousandth-unit amounts to integer USD notionals. It maps crossing-mode even IDs to passive quotes, odd IDs to FOK takes; deep-mode first 80% to passive quotes, final 20% to takes. One fifth of intents use priority. One and four disjoint router shards, same 200 trader sessions. Synthetic allocation caps cover the test; no real coins. Every signed intent is pre-signed outside the timers, then authenticated and queued during admission timing. Execution timing uses a virtual 450 ms clock, with all due events processed in local eligibility order; no socket, real sleep, real oracle requests, or settlement. Run 20k and 100k orders for each mode and shard count, report admission and execution p50/p99 separately, combined CPU throughput, fills, failures, retained quotes, pending fee obligations and RSS. The four-router arm executes shards sequentially on the same CPU; it measures partitioned workload, not parallel speedup. Unlike TL's sweep-and-settle benchmark, SHZ V1 is single-maker FOK, so the two engines have different fill behavior. Record measured upstream result and the mapped SHZ result without claiming exact parity.

Price-time ties use actual quote activation order after eligibility, not original admission order. Pending fill receipts retained in a bounded map keyed by taker intent ID. Tests exercise partial fee rounding, cancellation and expiry, cross-router reservations, stale and equivocal relay ticks, price-time after differentiated delay, authentic signatures and replay. No live funding or fee collection.
